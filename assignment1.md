# Data Reporting Critique: ProPublica & Anchorage Daily News

Article: [Lawless: One in three Alaska villages have no local police]([url](https://www.adn.com/alaska-news/lawless/2019/05/16/lawless-one-in-three-alaska-villages-have-no-local-police/))

# Clarity and Accuracy 

Central Insight (5/5): The central insight was immediately identifiable, one-third of Alaska's communities lack any local law enforcement, leaving vulnerable populations completely unprotected.
Headline: Yes, the insight was stated in the headline ("One in three Alaska villages have no local police").
Data Visualization(2/5): There are images throughout the article highlighting Anchorage and people that live there. There is one chart that shows the number of cities (map of Alaska) with no local law enforcement. In addition, there is a link to another article about how they tallied the areas with no local law enforcement.
Data Accuracy: The data appears highly accurate and sourced. The reporters manually found their data and asked more than 560 traditional councils, tribal corporations, and city governments about their officers.
  *Areas of question:* None based on methodology, though tracking exact real-time turnover of Village Public Safety Officers is likely difficult.
  *Sources to check:* Alaska Department of Public Safety, Alaska Police Standards Council
  *Explanations:* Yes, the reporters clearly explained the tiered system of Alaska law enforcement (State troopers vs. VSPOs vs. Village/Tribal police)

  # Quality, Type, Color, and Creativity 

  Overall Quality (5) - The execution is great, it seamlessly transitions between the macro (statewide budget cuts, lawsuits) and the micro (Annie Reed's daily life, home invasion).
  Type and Color (4) - The typography is stark and easily readable, drawing the eye to crucial points. I think the numbers and stats should be bolded or italicized to make it more distinguishable. 
  Creativity (5) - The piece uses jarring anecdotal details, like villagers duct-taping an active shooter, or using "blue tickets" for banishment, to illustrate the human cost of the missing resources. 

# Story Pitch: D.C. 

Analyzing the distribution of D.C. Department of Parks and Recreation (DPR) grants and their correlation into neighborhood public safety outcomes.

Angle: How equitably are municipal community grants (such as the Rec for All micro-grants) distributed across the District, and is there a measurable correlation between localized funding for recreational programming and neighborhood public safety metrics in heavily impacted areas like Ward 7?

# Data Availability:
*Grant Data:* D.C. Open Data portal and DPR press releases detailing the dollar amounts and recipient organizations of recent grant cycles.

*Crime Data:* Metropolitan Police Department (MPD) publicly available crime statistics filtered by Ward and Advisory Neighborhood Commission (ANC) boundaries.

*Relevance/Newsworthy:* As the District continues to debate the most effective strategies for curbing youth crime, analyzing whether non-policing community investments actually reach the neighborhoods with the highest safety needs is highly relevant to current city council budget discussions.

# Potential Sources:
- Ward 7 community advocates and ANC commissioners.
- Directors of local youth and recreational programs who applied for or received DPR funding.
- D.C. Department of Parks and Recreation officials overseeing the grant disbursement process.
