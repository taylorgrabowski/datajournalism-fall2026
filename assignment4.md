## Visualization 1: Taylor Swift tours

**Original chart:** [Taylor Swift Tours Data Set](https://makeovermonday.vercel.app/dataset/taylor-swift-tours)
**My redesign:** [Taylor Swift Redesign](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1YV-uq9KrpgzAI2UoJN_68iIBRHAodG4Tl95_MIzMhJ8/edit?usp=sharing)

**Changes I made from the original:**
- **Changed the chart type from a timeline to a horizontal bar chart.** The original shows when albums and tours happened. A bar chart makes it easy to compare the share of each tour's songs that came from the newest album.
- **Used one color for the bars.** The original used a different color for every album. I Just used one color for all of them.
- **Removed the year column, the arrows, and the album and tour headers.** They weren’t needed because my chart is about songs, not dates. Each bar has the tour name written right on it, so the reader doesn’t have to look anywhere else.
- **Replaced the question title with a headline that states the finding, and added a subhead.** The original title asks a question and leaves the reader to work out the answer. My headline gives the takeaway, and the subhead says what the bars measure.
- **Annotated the key data points.** I labeled the 1989 Tour as the highest (14 of 18 songs) and the Eras Tour as the outlier (7 of 43 songs from Midnights; 43 songs from 9 albums, with no album over 8). This guides the reader to the story.
- **Labeled each bar with its percentage and left out gridlines and an axis.** The labels make the values clear, and removing gridlines reduces clutter.

## Visualization 2: AI job risk

**Original chart:** [AI Risk Rankings Data Set](https://makeovermonday.vercel.app/dataset/ai-risk-rankings)
**My redesign:** [AI Risk Rankings Redesign](https://docs.google.com/document/d/1WiAUtnufDwvyd0R7U8ki19MU2oM7eLmIQ40_OGi0c6I/edit?usp=sharing))

**Changes I made from the original:**
- **Changed the chart type from two tables to a dot chart with average dots.** The original made the reader scan 30 rows of numbers. A range plot shows each group's wages as a line and its average as a dot, so the gap between the groups is visible at a glance.
- **Put both groups on one shared wage axis.** The original split them into separate "Highest Risk" and "Lowest Risk" boxes, which made them hard to compare. On one axis, the lines and dots sit side by side.
- **Summarized 30 rows as two averages.** The original listed every job, which buried the pattern. The averages state the main finding directly.
- **Removed the rank numbers, the red and green risk badges, the colored circles in the headers, and the employment column.** They repeated information from the wage comparison. The risk scores are now given as a range under each group name (82 to 96 and 13 to 23).
- **Added a headline and subhead.** The original had no headline. Mine states the finding, and the subhead says what the lines and dots show.
- **Annotated the key comparison.** The note says the best-paid high-risk job ($55K) still pays less than the lowest-paid low-risk job ($59K). This shows that the two groups' wages don't overlap.
